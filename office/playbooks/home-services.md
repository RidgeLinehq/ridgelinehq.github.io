# Home services playbook

Read by the crew when client.industry = "home-services" (plumbers, HVAC, electricians, cleaning services, landscapers, handyman). The customer is a homeowner or landlord with something broken, dirty or overgrown, who types "[plumber] near me" into Google while standing next to the problem and asks ChatGPT "who is a good [plumber] in [Town] that can come today?" They want a call back, a price they can trust and someone who shows up when they said.

Site promise (lead page header): "Service requests with the job, the address and the timing, so you can call back first."

client.services from onboarding says which trade. A company that does two (plumbing and HVAC is common) takes the first trade's primary category and the second trade's best category as a secondary. Hedge any Google category you have not seen: "if it shows when you type it". Never a number of leads or a ranking promised; reviews only from real customers; every lead opted in.

No answer: leave a 10-second voicemail with your name and the earliest window, then text the same line from your own phone. "Business / rental" leads want an invoice by email and a repeat contact; ask who signs off before you quote.

**Call-back script (owner's voice, 3 sentences):** "Hi [Name], this is [Owner] at [Business], you just asked us about [a clogged kitchen drain] at [Street]. If there is water on the floor right now, shut the valve under the sink and I will move you up the list."

## 3. Review growth

**When to ask:** plumber, HVAC, electrician, handyman: the tech asks at the door when the customer signs off, and the email goes the next morning. Cleaner: after the second or third recurring visit (not the first), or the same evening after a one-time deep clean. Landscaper: after the first mow they can see from the window, or after a project is walked and signed. One ask per customer, never a second inside 180 days.

**Review request email (under 90 words, sent by the reply desk on behalf of [Business], one link):**

> Subject: How did [Business] do?
>
> Hi [First name],
>
> Thanks for having [Business] out for your [water heater] on [Street] this week. If [Tech] did a good job, would you take a minute to leave a Google review? It helps other [Town] homeowners pick someone who shows up.
>
> [review link]
>
> Thank you,
> [Owner], [Business]
>
> Reply 'stop' and we won't email you again.

**Crew script (truck or door, one line):** "If everything's working the way you wanted, a quick Google review helps us a lot; the link is on your invoice."

**Where the QR card goes:** printed on the invoice next to the total; a magnet on the [furnace / water heater / panel door] with the QR and the emergency number; cleaners and landscapers leave a card on the counter or the mailbox after the visit; a sticker inside the van door for the tech to show.

**Reply to a 5-star review:** "Thank you, [First name]. Glad the [new water heater on [Street]] is doing its job. Call us any time something acts up."

**Reply to a 3-star review:** "Thank you for the honest feedback, [First name]. We want every [Town] job to end the way you expected, so please call me directly at [phone] and we will sort out [the arrival window / the invoice] together."

## 6. Compliance notes

- **License numbers:** plumbing, HVAC and electrical are licensed in most states; put "[State] license #[number]" on the page, in the description and on the invoice when the state requires it, and never write "licensed" without a number we have seen. Cleaners, landscapers and handymen: "insured" only if their policy is real; "bonded" only if they hold a bond.
- **Emergency pricing honesty:** the after-hours fee or trip charge is stated on the page and in Q&A #1 before anyone calls; "free estimate" is never written where a diagnostic fee applies.
- **Estimate vs quote:** "estimate" means the price can change once the work is open and the customer is told before it does; "quote" means a fixed price in writing. The page says "written estimate" unless the owner holds to fixed prices, then "written quote".
- **Background checks:** "background-checked" or "drug-tested" appears only if the owner confirms it is done for every tech and cleaner; "uniformed" only if true.
- **Handyman scope:** no plumbing, gas, electrical or HVAC work beyond what [State] allows without a license, and no job above the state's handyman dollar limit where one exists; the services list and the page stay inside that line.
- **Reviews:** real customers only, one link for everyone, no discounts for reviews, no "5 stars please". A request goes only for a job that is finished and paid.
- **Leads:** the crew sends the lead to the owner; it never replies to the post, never texts a stranger, never looks a person up. A "Business / rental" lead is still one person asking; it is not a list.

## 7. The 3 fixes we'd make first

1. **Hours with the emergency branch.** A plumber or HVAC company marked Closed at 5 pm loses the night calls it would have answered. Set the real hours, add "Open 24 hours" only if a person answers, and put the emergency line and fee in the description and Q&A #1. 2 minutes.
2. **Primary category and the services list.** "Contractor" or "Home improvement" with an empty services list says nothing to Google or ChatGPT. Set the trade's primary category and paste its services row. 5 minutes.
3. **The description with the license number, then the review ask.** Paste the 750-character description with the town, the nearby towns and the license number; the same week the owner sends us the emails of the last ten finished jobs and the review requests go out. 5 minutes plus one reply.

## 8. What a good first month looks like

- **Week 1:** Manager access granted; hours (with the emergency branch), categories, services, description and Q&A live; first ten photos up; the lead page live with the owner's phone as the big button and opened once to check it.
- **Week 2:** the owner sends the last ten finished jobs' emails; review requests go out at the rules' pace; the QR card is on the invoice template and in the van; the first Google post is up.
- **Week 3:** first review replies drafted and posted; the weekly visibility check records what ChatGPT and Gemini said word for word; the first lead alerts with the follow-up tip have landed (every plan), and the owner has called one back within the hour; Pro: the first Lead Finder leads.
- **Week 4:** the monthly check-in asks which leads became jobs; the report shows the real counts, zeros included, the AI answers quoted, and next month's three bullets.
- **Signals we want (not numbers):** the owner pasted the fixes within a week; new reviews name the town, the tech and the job ("water heater", "[Street]"); the profile check finds the new description on the copy sites; "Emergency, today" leads get a call back inside the hour; the services list matches what people actually ask for on the lead page.
