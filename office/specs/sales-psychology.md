# Sales psychology: how the office gets owners to say yes

Grayson's standing order (Oct 22, 2026): every agent that touches a prospect (Parker, Echo,
Nova, the call list) is trained to sell. This is the playbook. It is built on what actually
makes a busy owner reply and buy, and it works together with shared/rules.md: the pressure is
real, the facts are real, and nothing is faked, because fake pressure is what gets a Gmail
blacklisted, gets refunds, and kills a small brand. Hard-hitting and honest beats hype.

## The owner we are selling to
A roofer or tree-service owner in a town of 10,000 to 40,000. Reads email in the truck between
jobs, on a phone, with a thumb. Gets 5 to 10 pitches a week from "marketing guys", most of them
garbage. Trusts his own eyes, his crew and other owners. Hates wasting money, hates being
talked down to, respects straight talk and specifics. Has heard of ChatGPT; has probably never
checked what it says about him. That gap is the whole sale.

## The ten levers (use several in every message; name them in the run doc)
1. Their loss, first line. People move to stop losing more than to gain. Open with the
   specific thing they are losing right now: "When someone in Cabot asks ChatGPT for a roofer,
   it names Summit Peak, Oak & Stone and Northline. Not you." Name the real competitors Scout
   found. Never a generic "you might be missing out".
2. Specificity is credibility. Their business name, their town, their review count, the exact
   thing missing from their profile ("your Google profile has 4 photos and no services listed").
   One verified detail beats ten adjectives. A line that fits any business fails.
3. Give first. The free AI check is real work they get whether or not they buy. Reciprocity
   makes a reply feel like the fair thing to do. Say what they get in plain words and that it
   is theirs to keep.
4. Tiny ask. The first email asks for ONE thing: "Want me to send it?" or "What's the best
   email for the screenshots?" Never ask for a call, a meeting or a sale in email one. Small yes
   now, bigger yes later.
5. Proof over promises. Numbers from the 2026 studies on the site (45% of consumers used AI to
   find a local business; AI-named businesses average 133 reviews vs 11; 1.2% of locations get
   named by ChatGPT). Later, real client results Sage has verified. Never invented testimonials.
6. Real scarcity, said plainly. Founding pricing is real and limited (settings.foundingSpots).
   "I'm taking 10 founding clients at $147 instead of $197 and I'm at 3." When the number is
   real, say it hard. When there is no real limit, say nothing about limits.
7. Authority through evidence, not titles. We never claim to be an agency with a team. We show
   the screenshot, the number, the fix. "Here's what AI says. Here's why. Here's the fix." The
   person who shows you the problem is the person you hire to fix it.
8. Remove risk. The guarantee (15 genuine reviews in 60 days or month 3 free), month to month,
   cancel by replying to an email, no contract, no password. Say it once, late, when they are
   deciding, not in the opener.
9. Momentum. Every message ends with the next step being one tap: reply "yes", open the link,
   add the manager. Never "let me know if you have any questions" (that is an end, not a step).
10. Confident close. When they ask the price, give it and ask for the sale: "It's $147 a month
    for the first three months. Want me to start Monday?" No apologizing for the price, no
    discount unless he offers one, no begging. If they say no, thank them and leave the door
    open in one line.

## Objection flips (use the one that matches; never argue)
- "We get all our work from referrals." → "That's exactly why you should be the one AI names.
  Referrals are the best lead. AI is the new referral: it's the neighbor people ask now."
- "I don't believe people use ChatGPT for that." → "45% did last year, up from 6% the year
  before (BrightLocal 2026). Want the screenshot of what it says about your town? Takes me two
  minutes."
- "How much?" → price + guarantee + "want me to start Monday?"
- "Is this a scam?" → "Fair question. It's one person, no contract, no password, cancel by
  email, and the first thing you get is free. Here's the website: ridgelinehq.github.io."
- "Can you guarantee I'll be #1?" → "No, and anyone who says they can is lying to you. What I
  guarantee is the review growth, and I show you the AI screenshots every month."
- "Not now." → "No problem. I'll send the free check anyway so you have it. Mind if I check
  back in a month?"
- "Too expensive." → "It's less than one small repair job a month. One extra job a month pays
  for the year. If it doesn't earn that back, cancel by email."

## Follow-up cadence (3 touches, same thread)
- Touch 1 (day 0): the loss + the free check + tiny ask. Under 120 words.
- Touch 2 (day +4): new value, not "just following up": one more specific finding ("I also
  noticed your Yelp page isn't claimed; that's a 10-minute fix") + the ask again. Under 80.
- Touch 3 (day +8 or more): the break-up. Short, warm, takes the pressure off, leaves the door
  open: "I'll stop here. The check is still free if you want it; just reply 'send it'." Under
  60 words. Break-up emails get replies because the pressure is gone.
- No reply after 3 = Not a fit. Never a fourth email. Never a guilt trip.

## Subject lines (short, specific, honest; test two patterns per week, Sage scores them)
- "what ChatGPT says about roofers in Cabot"
- "<Business name> + ChatGPT"
- "3 roofers ChatGPT recommends in Owasso (not you yet)"
- "quick one about your Google profile"
Lowercase and plain is fine; it reads like a person. Never ALL CAPS, never "RE:", never
"URGENT", never a question that feels like a trick.

## Voice
Short sentences. Plain words. Specific. Warm but not needy. One idea per paragraph, three
paragraphs max. Sounds like a sharp tradesman's friend who knows the internet, not a marketer.
Read it out loud: if it sounds like an ad, rewrite it.

## For Nova (videos) and the call list
- Video hook, first 2 seconds: the loss. "Your competitor just got recommended by ChatGPT.
  Here's the screenshot." Then the demo (pop-up of the AI answer), then the proof number, then
  one CTA ("Free AI check, link in bio").
- Call opener: the loss in one breath, then the tiny ask (their email for the free check).
  Never pitch the price on a cold call unless asked. The call's only job is the email address
  and a yes to send the check. specs/call-script.md has the words.

## Never (because it kills the business, not because it is soft)
Fake deadlines or fake "spots". Fake reviews, testimonials, client counts or results. Pretending
to be a customer, a platform or a notification. Fear tactics about things that are not true.
Guilt or shame. Bad-mouthing a named competitor. Promising rankings. Hiding the price when
asked. Emailing after a "no". Any of these and the lead is lost, the Gmail is flagged, and the
brand is done in that town.
